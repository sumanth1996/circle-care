# Practice Round (Synthetic)

Notion stories: CC-2, CC-3, CC-4 (practice notes) · CC-5 (practice synthesis)
Status: PRACTICE ROUND, SYNTHETIC, 2026-11-16. No real person was interviewed and no one was contacted. Every "participant" below is made up from published research and the personas. Nothing here is a finding, and no hypothesis is supported or rejected by it.

## Why this exists

CC-2 to CC-5 ask for real interviews: 6 or more elders, caregivers and guardians, then journey maps checked with at least 5 participants. The product owner asked that nobody be contacted, so those stories stay open. This round does the part that needs no people:

1. It dry-runs the three [interview guides](02-interview-guide-elders.md) against made-up participants, to find gaps before a real session.
2. It reads published caregiving research and says, for each hypothesis, what that research already suggests.
3. It fills the [synthesis kit](11-synthesis-kit.md) tables once, as a worked example, so the first real round has a model to follow.

A practice answer is the agent's guess at what someone like this might say, written to exercise the guide and the note template. It is never evidence. Delete or overwrite every practice row once real notes exist.

## Method

- **Participants:** 18 synthetic profiles, IDs PR-E1 to PR-E6, PR-C1 to PR-C6 and PR-G1 to PR-G6. The PR prefix keeps them apart from the real IDs (E1, C1, G1…) in the [recruiting tracker](09-recruiting-tracker.csv). Each profile follows the screener §2 quota mix.
- **Answers:** written from the personas, the [journey maps](05-journey-maps.md) and the sources below. Each row names the sources it leans on.
- **Concept tests:** not run. The tasks need a real person holding the research phone, so every task score is blank.
- **Sources:** public pages only, read on 2026-11-16. Where a source is dated, outside the US or only an opinion piece, the table says so.

## What the dry run found in the guides

These are real findings about the documents, not about people. Each needs the product owner's call before the first session.

1. **The guardian guide has no time for consent.** Its sections add up to the full 70 minutes, and consent (fieldwork plan §4, step 1) comes before §1. The elder and caregiver guides leave 3 and 2 minutes. Suggested fix: drop T5 and T9 by default, or book guardians for 75 minutes.
2. **CC-4 asks for 6 guardians; the screener's minimum is 5**, and the guardian hypotheses are written as "3+ of 5". Either the story or the screener should change.
3. **CC-3 asks for family and professional caregivers; the caregiver guide is for professionals only**, and family helpers are a 0 to 2 bonus quota. PR-C6 shows that a family helper's answers (training, medicines at home) have no section or hypothesis to land in.
4. **Elder §4 asks "Has anyone ever known something about you that you wish they hadn't?"** That can draw out a disclosure of harm. The guide already links the safety protocol; the moderator should expect it at that question.
5. **Older elders are often not smartphone users** (S6: 17% at 80+). The screener's "at least 2 who use a smartphone only for calls or texts" matters, and T1 to T8 may mostly measure first use of a phone app.
6. **No note-template field for who taught them a task.** S1's training gap (22%) suggests asking caregivers and family helpers who showed them each medical task.

## Practice synthesis (to be confirmed with real notes)

### Elders: top 5 pains and 3 jobs to be done

Pains, in no order of evidence:
1. Repeated check-in calls feel like being monitored.
2. Not knowing who reads what the aide writes.
3. Doubt about whether a dose was taken.
4. Decisions about their care made without asking them.
5. Phones and apps built for younger eyes and hands.

Jobs to be done:
1. "Let my family know I'm fine without having to say it every day."
2. "Help me keep my medicines straight without someone standing over me."
3. "Let me decide who sees what about me."

### Caregivers: tools, workarounds and top pains

- **Tools:** the agency's own app (often with clock-in), personal texts, a paper notebook in the home, phone calls to pharmacies.
- **Workarounds:** texting the family on arrival as well as clocking in; writing notes at home after the shift; asking the family by text whether a medicine changed.
- **Top pains:** the same information entered in two or three places; medicine changes that arrive by text; unpaid time on updates; being left out of the care plan (S4).

### Guardians: decisions and alerts, ranked

Decisions guardians approve, most to least weighty in the practice round: medicine changes, care-schedule changes, who joins the circle, spending on care. Alerts they need, ranked: SOS, a missed dose the caregiver hasn't fixed, a missed visit, low medicine supply, a change in eating or mood.

## Does the MVP scope still hold?

Nothing in the desk research argues against the MVP. Two points deserve a look before real sessions:

- **Elders 80 and over may never use the app themselves** (S6). The guardian and caregiver sides then carry most of the value, and the elder's controls (pause sharing, *My day* privacy) may be set by someone else on their behalf. Real interviews should check who would actually hold the phone.
- **Family helpers do hands-on medical tasks with little training** (S1). Circle Care has no family-helper role beyond the chat. That is a possible gap, not a decision.
