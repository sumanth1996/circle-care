## 3. Before each session (run sheet)

- [ ] The consent form was sent at booking and the participant has had time to read it.
- [ ] The research phone is charged, in a guest browser profile, with keyboard learning and suggestions off and the microphone blocked for the prototype site (screener §7).
- [ ] The practice app shows the demo and *Reset demo* was tapped after the last session.
- [ ] The note template for this persona is open under the participant ID (for example `E03`), with no name on it.
- [ ] The recording is set to save to the encrypted research folder (if the participant agrees to recording).
- [ ] The moderator has the safety protocol (§5) to hand.

## 4. During and after each session

1. Go through the consent outline together. Take a signature, or record verbal consent with the date. If the person can't consent for themselves, stop politely: we don't take proxy consent.
2. Follow the guide. Offer a break at least every 25 minutes for elders.
3. If the participant names a condition, medicine or doctor, write "[condition]", "[med]" or "[doctor]" instead.
4. If anything in §5 comes up, pause and follow the protocol.
5. After the session: tap *Reset demo*, clear the browser's site data and history for the prototype, and check the phone is back to the start.
6. Within 24 hours: tidy the notes, check they are de-identified, save them in the encrypted folder and tick the session in the tracker.
7. Send the incentive within 5 working days, and tick *Incentive sent*.
8. Share the de-identified notes with the agents for synthesis (see the [synthesis kit](11-synthesis-kit.md)).

## 5. Safety protocol for disclosures of harm (draft, needs review)

**Human action:** the research lead reviews and approves this protocol, ideally with someone experienced in adult protective work, before the first session. Until then, no interviews run.

This protocol covers a participant saying, or the moderator seeing, that an older adult is being neglected, abused, financially exploited, or is in an unsafe situation, or that the participant is in danger themselves.

1. **Immediate danger.** If someone is in danger right now (a fall with injury, a threat of violence, a medical emergency), stop the interview and call 911, or help the participant call. Stay on the line if you can.
2. **No immediate danger.** Pause the interview. Thank the person for telling you. Don't investigate, ask leading questions, or promise to keep it secret, because you may need to share it.
3. **Offer help.** Tell the participant how to reach the Adult Protective Services office in their state, and the Eldercare Locator for local services. The research lead keeps the current contact details for both on a printed card for every session; don't rely on an app during the call.
4. **Decide whether to report.** Some people (for example licensed professionals) must report suspected elder abuse in their state. The research lead decides, using the reviewed guidance for the participant's state, and makes any report. Don't report from the session itself unless the danger is immediate.
5. **Carry on or stop.** Ask whether the participant wants to continue, take a break or stop. They receive the incentive either way.
6. **Record.** Write only "safety protocol used" and the date against the participant ID in the tracker. Write the details only in the research lead's separate, restricted log, never in the notes or the repository.
7. **Debrief.** The moderator and the research lead talk within 24 hours. The moderator can step back from later sessions.
